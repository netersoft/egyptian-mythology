Egyptian mythology is both fascinating and complex. It is distinguished by several characteristics:
  • The multiple faces given to the gods because most of the deities were equipped with several modes of representation. The zoomorphic shape is probably the oldest, but very quickly it was added the purely anthropomorphic form. The composite form that combines a body human to animal head, or vice versa, is later
  • the importance given to symbols through animals,
  • the locality of most gods and the cycles that punctuate myths.
The deities were omnipresent in daily life of the Egyptian people and they sought in these deities, support and protection against the forces of evil.
Below are presented the main gods that make up the Egyptian pantheon:

Amemet

The goddess Amemet is represented with the back part of a hippopotamus, the part of a lion and the head of a crocodile. When weighing hearts in the afterlife, she "devoured" those of those found guilty.

Amun

Chief of the gods in the New Kingdom, Amun looked like a man wearing a headdress decorated with two long feathers, or that of a ram or a goose. His wife, Mut, he and their son Khonsu constitute the Theban triad, the sacred family of Thebes. Amun came to acquire considerable importance, but never became a state god. He was associated with the god Re and venerated in the form of the god Amun-Re.

Amun-Re

Amun-Re is one of the forms of the solar god and is sometimes depicted as a sphinx or a being human with a hawk's head. The solar disk is its symbol. The word "Amun" means "the hidden" or "the hidden character of the divinity", while "Re" or "Ra" means "the sun" or the divinity manifested by the power of the sun. The god Amun-Re embodies these two ideas: the ever-present invisible power and the bright light of the divine force that ensures life. To trace the origins of Amun-Re, we must go back to the Old Kingdom, to Heliopolis, where the god Re appeared for the first time as the main manifestation of the god solar. Re is represented with the head of a falcon surmounted by the solar disk when he crosses the sky, and with a ram's head when he completes his nocturnal journey in the world lower. This local god rose to the rank of national god, which led to the erection of solar temples all over the country. Under the 4th dynasty, the pharaohs began to get together to consider as incarnations of this god. Later, during the Middle Kingdom, when Amun became the main god, Re was merged with him to become Amun-Re.

Anubis

Anubis was a jackal-headed deity who presided over the embalming and accompanied the dead kings in the afterlife. When kings appeared before Osiris to be judged, Anubis placed their hearts on one of the trays of a balance, and a feather (representing Maat) on the other. The god Thoth recorded the result, on which depended the king's right to access the afterlife. Anubis is the son of Osiris and Nephthys.

Aten

Primordial cosmic god, Aten is the solar god as creator, the substance from which is drawn all of creation. He is the lord of the universe. In its human form, it represents the king of Egypt, who wears the double crown of Egypt.

Bastet

Pussy Goddess, she symbolizes the most important aspects protectors of motherhood, as opposed to the aggressive Sekhmet with a lion's head. Shown with a body of a woman and a pussy head, she often wears a sistre.

Bes

This dwarf god, whose lippu face looks like a grotesque mask, often wears instruments musicals, knives or the hieroglyph representing protection. Despite his appearance, he is the protector of the family and is associated with sexuality and childbirth.

Chu & Tefnut

Chu is a male god twinned with his sister, Tefnut. Together, they personify two fundamental principles of human existence. Chu symbolizes dry air and strength of conservation. Tefnut embodies the humid and corrosive air that causes change, creating the concept of time. Chu and Tefnout are the children of Re ( or of Tem, a form of the solar god), a primordial cosmic god, who has generated the elements of the universe.

Geb & Nut

These two gods are the counterpart of each other. Geb, an earthly god, who represents the emerged earth, is the husband of Nut, a celestial goddess who symbolizes the sky, inverted image of the sea. Nut is represented as a woman with the hieroglyph of his name on his head or like a woman with an arched body covered with stars. They are the children of Chu and Tefnut.

Hapi

Hapi, the one who brings abundance, ensured the annual flooding of the Nile which fertilized the land, allowing it to be cultivated. This divinity was represented as a man with hair adorned with plants holding an offering table furnished with products of the earth.

Hathor

This goddess can take three aspects: that of a woman holding a necklace-menat and the solar disc and carrying a crown formed by the horns of a cow, the one of a woman with cow's ears, or a cow's. She is the girl of Re and the wife of Horus, the heavenly falcon-god. It is also considered as the mother of every reigning king (just like the goddess Isis). The personality of Hathor is twofold: under its vengeful aspect, it took on the leonine appearance of the goddess Sekhmet and tried to destroy humanity after the revolt described in the myth of creation; in its bovine form, it is associated with sexuality, joy and music.

Horus

Horus, the falcon-headed god, is well known in Egypt. Became one of the the most common symbols of Egypt. Horus is the son of Osiris and Isis, the divine child of the sacred triad. He is one of the many gods associated with the falcon. Its name means "the one above". and also "the one who is far away". The falcon has been venerated since ancient times as a cosmic divinity. His body represented the sky, and his eyes the sun and the moon. Horus is represented as a falcon wearing a crown with a cobra or the double crown of Egypt. The snake naja (uræus) only the gods and the pharaohs wore on the forehead symbolizes light and royalty. Its purpose is to function to protect the person from danger. When Horus was a child, his father was killed by his brother Seth. To protect its son of all danger, Isis hid him in the marshes of the Nile, where she protected him from poisonous snakes, scorpions, crocodiles and wild animals. As he grew up, he learned to protect himself and became strong enough to fight Seth and claim his just inheritance, the throne of Egypt. As a result, Horus is associated with the title of king. He personifies the divine and royal power. The kings believed that they were descended from Horus, who was considered to be the first divine king of Egypt.

Isis

Isis embodies the power of love to defeat death. She brought her brother and husband, Osiris, back to life and saved her son Horus from certain death. She is represented the head surmounted by the hieroglyph meaning "throne"; from the New Kingdom, she sometimes wore a solar disc flanked by cow horns. She is also often depicted mourning the death of her husband. and to breastfeed her son.

Khnum

God with a ram's head and horizontal horns, he is often depicted as to shape humans on his turn. Khnum has emerged from two caves in the world underground in the Nun Ocean. He was the god of the first Nile cataract, in Upper Egypt, and ensured fertility by directing half of the river's water to the south and the other half to the north .

Khonsu

Son of the Theban couple Amun and Mut, he is a lunar god often represented with a falcon's head topped by a crescent moon and a disc lunar. It also often looks like a young man with a wick of hair falling on the side of the face, the head surmounted by a crescent moon and a lunar disc.

Maat

Maat is the goddess of truth and justice. The iconography the represents in the form of a woman with a headband around her head adorned with an ostrich feather.

Min

God having human or mummy form, Min is represented with a erect phallus and a crown surmounted by two feathers. In his right hand, which is raised, he is holding a plague. He is the protector fertility and travel in the desert.

Mut

Wife of the Theban god Amun, she was first represented in the form of of a vulture, then, later, under that of a woman. This goddess, her husband Amun and their son, Khonsu, constitute the Theban Triad, the sacred family of Thebes.

Neith

Goddess associated with war and weaving, she is with Isis, Nephthys and Selkis one of the important characters of the funeral service. She is usually depicted wearing the red crown of Lower Egypt.

Nekhbet

Represented as a vulture, Nekhbet was the main goddess of Upper Egypt, whose king she protected. Its counterpart was Ouadjet, the goddess-cobra.

Nephtys

Nephthys is the daughter of Nut, the sister of Isis and the wife of Seth, the god of disorder. It is however to Osiris, from whom she had a child, Anubis, that her loyalty goes. When the Seth found out who the child's father was, he murdered Osiris, and Nephthys joined Isis in his search for Osiris' body. Like her sister Isis, she helps the dead and protect them. She is represented as a woman and wears on her head the hieroglyphic of his name.

Osiris

The first mention of Osiris, one of the main Egyptian gods, appears in texts funerals of the Pyramid era, when the use of mummification began (2400 BC). J.-C.). He presided over the gods who determined the fate of kings when they died. He has the appearance of a mummified man wearing a high white crown adorned with two ostrich feathers. According to Egyptian mythology, Osiris was murdered by his brother Seth and then brought back to the life through the love of his sister-wife, Isis. This myth describes the destructive forces that have generated the process of mummification. Isis' love is the symbol of regeneration and the promise of eternal life. The cycle of destruction, death and rebirth was repeated every year during the annual flooding of the Nile, the river that provided the elements essential to life and gave birth to one of the first civilizations. Osiris is also likened to the miracle of the Nile and the rich harvests it allowed. Osiris, Isis and their son, Horus, formed a sacred family: god, goddess and divine child.

Uadjet

She was the goddess-cobra whose murderous force protected the king of Lower Egypt. Sound southern counterpart was Nekhbet, the the goddess vulture.

Ptah

Creator God of the city of Memphis and husband of Sekhmet, Ptah is represented as a mummified man holding a ouas (a sceptre at the upper curved part with the animal head shape, and at the forked base).

Re

The solar god was considered as the central creative principle and original. The daily sunrise and sunset brought the tangible proof of the power of the sun to fall into the sky and to be reborn every morning in the eastern sky. It was Re who gave the Egyptians the concept of Maat - the principle of truth (law) and fair justice. This fundamental concept became the cornerstone of Egyptian civilization. The journey cosmic of the sun, symbolized by the beetle (dung beetle that pushes the disc through the sky), would continue as long as we continued to worship the solar god and Ma'at. When the other gods, the pre-eminence of Re was extended to other forms of the solar god - Chu, then Geb and finally Osiris.
On earth, the kings of the Old Kingdom were considered as the mortal incarnation of the solar god. In other words, the king was a god on earth, and by his actions, accomplished in respect of justice, he prevented the world from falling into chaos. The solar god is also known as Re-Horakhty ("Horus of the horizon") and of Tem (the "All"), the substance from which is pulled all of creation. Re-Horakhty is a god with a human body and a falcon head bearing a crown in the shape of a solar disc surrounded by a cobra, or a crown made of ram's horns and decorated with ostrich feathers. Tem is represented as King of Egypt and Lord of the universe and wears the double crown of Egypt. All these forms of the solar god are symbols of the promise of the resurrection, a response to the problem of human mortality. The cult of the sun has been perpetuated by Egyptian kings for centuries. They built pyramids (symbolizing the climbing ladder to the sun, or the oblique rays of it) and later on from temples dedicated to the solar gods. When a king died, his actions were judged in the afterlife by Osiris, one of the forms of the solar god who ruled the lower world. If his actions were considered as the king was transformed into a form of the solar god.

Sekhmet

Sekhmet has the appearance of a woman with a lion's head surmounted by a solar disc and a uraeus (cobra). She was an important goddess of the Theban capital during the New Kingdom (1550-1070 a v. AD). Its name means "the one who is powerful", and as such, she embodies the aggressive aspects of female goddesses.

Selkis

Selkis is usually represented as a woman with a scorpion on the head. Its name means "the one that allows the throat to breathe". She is the protector of the falcon-headed canopic, and with three other goddesses, Isis, Nephthys and Neith, she keeps the sarcophagi and royal canopic chests.

Seth

Seth is the offspring of Geb and Nut. As a god of the disorder, he is responsible for the murder of his brother, Osiris. In the Egyptian dualist concept of the cosmos, Seth matched Horus, the god who ruled the earth, ensuring its order and stability. Seth is a divinity with a rounded animal head, with large ears with square ends and tail standing up like an arrow. We haven't identified the animal it represents. It is often depicted with a human body and an elongated bird's head, which makes him look like the god Thoth.

Sobek

The name of this crocodile-headed god means "the one that makes you pregnant or fertile. At his place of worship in Kom Ombo, he shared a temple with Horus. He was later integrated into the cult of Amun, being worshipped as a manifestation of the solar god, Re.

Thoth

Thoth is the messenger of the solar god. He is the god of knowledge and wisdom, the inventor of writing and science and the protector of the scribes. He is represented as a human being with an ibis head, a baboon, or the moon.

Tueris

Goddess who protected women in childbirth, Tueris is depicted with a hippopotamus head, the legs of a lion, and the back and tail of a crocodile. Her heavy breasts and prominent belly indicate that she is pregnant.
