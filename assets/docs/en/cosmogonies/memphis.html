The history of the Memphis myth is a unique and exciting one. In 1805, the British Museum hosts in its Egyptian collection, a rectangular stone, damaged because it has served in a grain millstone from an Egyptian peasant. It contains 63 columns of finely engraved texts. But at that time, no one could read hieroglyphics. However, this stone contains a religious text of exceptional importance. This is the memphite theology telling the creation of the world according to the cosmogony of Memphis, the ancient capital south of modern Cairo. The monument dates back to the 25th century. dynasty and more precisely the reign of Chabaka. As in other myths, the Word is at the heart of everything but here the word materializes what it expresses.

The demiurge of Memphis is neither Atum nor Thot, but Ptah ( "sculptor of the earth", the one who opens). "Ptah conceives the world through the thought of his heart and gives it life through the magic of his Word. Some documents also explain that Ptah conceives through Thought (represented by Horus) and creates by the Word (represented by Thoth).
