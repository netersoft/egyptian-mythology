In the Theban cosmogony, it is the god Amun who occupies the place of demiurge. This cosmogony brings together elements from all the others: Heliopolitan, memphite, and hermopolitan. This gives it a universal character, rather practical when you want to impose yourself all over Egypt. It is said that at the dawn of the world, the winged serpent Kematef, the one who accomplishes his time, emerged from the Nun to the very place of Thebes. This winged snake became Amun later on. In another version, Amun is a goose that lays the cosmic egg. What is certain is that Amun put to the Irta world, the one who made the earth. Irta undertook to forge the universe: he begat the earth, and the eight main gods (ogdoad). They went to Heliopolis to bring forth Tem, and Ptah. Exhausted, they returned to Thebes and fell asleep forever, alongside Amun and Irta.
Originally a small deity of no importance, the cult of Amun never ceased to gain importance over time, becoming the creative divinity Amun-Ra under the New Kingdom.

The mystery of Kematef and the Theban Ogdoad

By appropriating the Hermopolitan Ogdoad, Theban theologians sought to show that Amun existed even before the eight primordial deities, since it was he who was said to have given birth to them before they travelled on to Heliopolis. This late but skilful theological manoeuvre allowed Thebes to claim mythological precedence over every other holy city in Egypt, even though it had originally been no more than an unremarkable town.

The rise of the cult of Amun

Under the New Kingdom, the temple of Karnak became the largest religious complex ever built in Egypt, enlarged by generation after generation of pharaohs eager to win the favour of the dynastic god. Amun's priesthood amassed such wealth and political influence there that it at times rivalled royal power itself. As a god of air and invisible breath, Amun embodied a fundamental paradox of Egyptian theology: a hidden creator, whose presence could only be sensed through its effects, unlike Re or Aten, both visible in the solar disc.
