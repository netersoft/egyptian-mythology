In the very beginning there was only a primordial ocean called the Nun; darkness reigned and nothing existed. Out of this primordial ocean rose Atum, or Tem ("He who is complete", "He who is the totality", "He who is and who is not"), the first of the gods, who brought himself into being simply by becoming aware of his own existence. The god then imagined a mound of earth, the Benben, in the middle of the waters, and it appeared so that he could stand upon it. Having no companion, Atum united with himself, and two children were born of this act: a son, Shu ("vital breath", god of dry air), and a daughter, Tefnut (goddess of moist air).
This birth had considerable consequences, for Shu is Life: he is life itself, and he is also light. Tefnut is the first goddess, the first female being of Creation, and the wife of her brother Shu. She embodies the warmth of the sun and is a personification of Maat. Maat appears very early on, as soon as the Creator becomes aware of himself and sets the celestial mechanism in motion. Maat is a metaphysical principle that governs order: she is the Order that holds Good and Evil in balance and keeps Chaos at bay. For the Egyptians, Good and Evil were inseparable; neither could exist and thrive without the other.

The birth of the sky and the earth

From the union of Shu and Tefnut two children were born: Geb, the earth, and Nut, the sky. From Atum would then come the sun, which later became Re, the sun god, but it could not yet move freely. So, to guarantee balance and cosmic order, Atum ordered Shu to hold the Earth (Geb) and the Sky (Nut) apart: the Universe was thus created, and little by little the Nun was pushed back beyond the edges of the newborn world.

The birth of mankind

The demiurge Atum gives life to human beings just as he gave it to the first gods: mankind did not appear on earth by itself. The Coffin Texts (funerary texts of the Middle Kingdom, 2000-1780 BC) mention this creation several times, most notably in this short passage: "men are the tears of my eye". One of the most common accounts of the creation of mankind holds that humans were born from the tears of the demiurge's eye, and that Shu breathed life into them.

Nut's children

One of the consequences of the appearance of the sun and the sky was Re's 360-day solar course, and with it the creation of day and night. But as you know, a year has 365 days, so five days were missing. These last days are part of Creation too. Nut, who was pregnant, could not give birth during the normal course of the sun, so a ruse had to be found. According to one legend, Thoth played a game against the Moon and won, creating five extra days that allowed Nut to give birth undisturbed.
These five days are known as the "epagomenal" days, from the Greek epagomenai hemerai, meaning "added days". In Egyptian cosmogony, they are identified with the five "children of Nut": Isis, Osiris, Seth, Nephthys and Horus the Elder (not to be confused with Horus, the son of Isis and Osiris). These five deities would reveal evil deeds to mankind. Osiris was born on the first day, Horus the Elder on the second, Seth on the third, Isis on the fourth and, finally, Nephthys on the last. The Egyptians considered these days ill-omened, especially the third, the day of Seth.

The Ennead

The Ennead is a group of nine divine beings conceived by the theologians of Heliopolis, embodying the forces needed for the creation of an ordered world: the demiurge Atum, his children Shu and Tefnut, his grandchildren Geb and Nut, and their descendants except Horus the Elder, namely the two couples Isis and Osiris, Nephthys and Seth.

The forces of chaos

When the creator became aware of himself, the Nun, let us remember, already held every ingredient needed for creation. Atum did not accomplish his task alone: serpents helped him. Yet these creatures became dangerous once the world existed, because they belonged to the Nun, the Chaos that threatens cosmic order. And when Re, the sun, leaves the daytime sky to enter the hours of the night, he must make a twelve-hour journey aboard his solar barque before being reborn at dawn. One of the sun's enemies is the giant serpent Apophis, born in the heart of the Nun. Uncreated, like Atum, Apophis cannot be destroyed; he can only be held in check.
