The history of pharaonic Egypt spans more than three thousand years. Historians divide it into great periods, which group together the thirty-one dynasties of kings. The dates, especially the earliest, are approximate.

Predynastic Period

Before 3100 BC. Communities settle along the Nile. Some gods, such as Neith or Min, are already worshipped.

Early Dynastic Period

c. 3100–2686 BC (1st and 2nd Dynasties). Upper and Lower Egypt are united under a single king. Hieroglyphic writing appears, Memphis becomes the capital and the king reigns as a Horus on earth.

Old Kingdom

2686–2181 BC (3rd to 6th Dynasties). The age of the pyramids, from Djoser’s at Saqqara to the great pyramids of Giza. The sun cult of Heliopolis prevails: from the 5th Dynasty on, every king bears the title “son of Re”. The Pyramid Texts are carved in the royal tombs.

First Intermediate Period

2181–2055 BC. Royal power collapses and the country splits up among rival princes.

Middle Kingdom

2055–1650 BC (11th and 12th Dynasties). Kings from Thebes, the Mentuhoteps, reunite the country. Amun begins his rise, Amenemhat III develops the Faiyum and the cult of Sobek, and private individuals are buried with the Coffin Texts.

Second Intermediate Period

1650–1550 BC. The Hyksos, who came from Asia, rule the Delta from Avaris, where they worship Seth. The princes of Thebes eventually drive them out.

New Kingdom

1550–1069 BC (18th to 20th Dynasties). The height of Egypt’s power. Amun-Re becomes the god of the state and Karnak the largest temple in the country. Kings are buried in the Valley of the Kings, whose tombs are decorated with the Amduat, and private individuals in tombs furnished with the Book of the Dead. Akhenaten imposes the cult of the Aten (c. 1353–1336 BC), before Tutankhamun restores the old gods. This is also the age of Amenhotep III, Seti I and Ramesses II.

Third Intermediate Period

1069–664 BC. Egypt is divided: the high priests of Amun rule Upper Egypt from Thebes. Kings from Nubia found the 25th Dynasty; one of them, Shabaka, has the Memphite theology carved on a granite slab around 710 BC.

Late Period

664–332 BC. The kings of Sais (26th Dynasty) restore past greatness, before the Persian invasions. The Greek historian Herodotus visits Egypt around 450 BC and describes its temples and festivals. Seth gradually becomes the god of Egypt’s enemies.

Ptolemaic Period

332–30 BC. Alexander the Great conquers Egypt; his successors, the Ptolemies, reign as pharaohs. They build the great temples still standing today: Edfu, Dendera, Kom Ombo and Philae.

Roman Period

30 BC – AD 395. Egypt becomes a Roman province. The cult of Isis spreads throughout the Empire, and Plutarch writes On Isis and Osiris around AD 100. The temple of Philae, the last sanctuary of the old gods, is not closed until the 6th century.
