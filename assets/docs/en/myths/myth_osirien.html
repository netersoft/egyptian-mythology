The birth of Nut's children

Re, the sun god, carefully observed the whole world as well as the Duat (the afterlife). One day, he discovered that Nut was pregnant after having a relationship with Geb. He entered a great anger and forbids the goddess to give birth. However, Nut had no choice: when she would come to term, her offspring would be born and she would be punished by Re. She searched in vain for hide, but the sun god saw everything, leaving nothing to chance. It was the god Thoth who came to his rescue because he was himself in love with Nut: so he would have done anything for her. He imagined a clever plan to deceive Re, which consisted of playing dice with the Moon. After a very long game, Thoth won and asked the Moon for a pledge: additional days in the year (epagomatic days) for which Re could not monitor what the gods were doing. That's how Nut was able to give birth her children without her having to endure Re's inquisitive gaze.
On the first day, she gave birth to Osiris, the heir to Geb and therefore the next king of the Earth. The second to see the day was Horus the elder, then the next day Set was born. The last two days the girls were born: Isis and Nephthys. It is also possible that the five children did not have the same father because Nut had many courtiers.

The duality Osiris and Seth

First son of Geb and Nout, the members of Osiris are golden, his headdress of lapis lazuli (semi-precious blue stone), his turquoise crown. He is extremely tall (4m50) even for a god and very strong. His intelligence is second to none, he even makes it his weapon by using the verb like no one else. His magnificence is such that for all the gods, he will impose himself as the king of them, after his father Geb. Its influence is such that it brings prosperity and happiness to all people in both countries and beyond its borders. His presence is such that no enemy will dare to stand in his way. Osiris is a god who gets everything he wants by word, so he succeeds in persuading his sister and wife Isis to stay with him after his infidelities with his other sister Nephthys, of whom he had a son, Anubis. Isis is also a very beautiful person, she fills the palace with a sparkling light and is respected like her husband when he is absent due to a civilizing mission such as the revelation of agriculture and from breeding to men. His tragic fate is the beginning of an unprecedented political crisis between the gods which will culminate in the ascension of the first pharaoh, Horus, to the throne of Egypt.

Seth is a very dark character and full of hatred and violence. His birth was particularly trying for his mother Nut since he raped her on her side, opening her, tearing her apart to get out faster. Seth is a small being with an outward appearance, red as the sand of the desert, it symbolizes infertility. He therefore had no children of his sister and married Nephthys; and therefore to avoid to reveal the infidelity she had with Osiris, she gave Anubis to Isis, who accepted him as her faithful guide and right hand man.

Osiris is a being who has no equivalent and his brother Seth, being his perfect opposite, will decide to kill him and plot against him because being excessively jealous.

Seth's trickery

To kill Osiris, Seth could not face him directly, he had to be deceived because this giant god was too powerful for anyone, his power was so great that it tended to be equivalent to that of the demiurge, the sun god and leader of the Ennead, Re. Seth invited the Queen of Ethiopia, Aso, whom he rallied to his macabre cause, to a sumptuous banquet where, among other things, all the Ennead was present. In the centre of the room was an imposing chest very well worked, covered with gold and precious stones. The trunk shone so brightly that everyone wanted to take it for themselves. Seth then organized a game where the chest would be the reward, In turn the guests lay down in it and if it were their size they would own it. Of course, the only one person who had the greatness to be the worthy possessor was Osiris. Before he had time to get up, 72 henchmen of Seth rushed to lock him up with large nails, sealed with molten lead. The servants of the vile brother took him away chest, in which Osiris rested, to the Nile to throw him there. The end of Osiris' reign had come. Some say that this reign lasted 400 years, others 28 years, in the image of a lunation because 28 human years are only 28 days for a god.

The consequences of a tragic death

When Osiris' death was announced, the whole world wavered, all the elements were unleashed, everything was threatening to return to the stadium of the primary chaos Even nature was depressed: the sun almost never rose, the moon was slow to rise, the earth was devastated. Men wept, so did the gods. But the tears produced were a source of life in contact with the dry soil, and by that the benefits of Osiris continued even after his death. Geb, in his pain, bled and the blood that came out of his nose dripped, and on contact with the ground he grew resin-rich pines. From the tears of Chu and Tefnut came the terebinth resin, from the tears of Re gush forth from the bees that would go and forage the flowers producing wax and honey. Re's sweat, exhausted by these disasters that he was trying to contain, the flax germinated and the cloth of the priests' clothes was woven. Ra's sputum and vomit were transformed in bitumen and papyrus. It is even said that Horus, not yet born, mourned his father's death and created the dry olibanum by leaving his tears touch the ground.

Isis' first quest

Isis, on the other hand, was in mourning. Her husband's death was a terrible ordeal that saddened her to the fullest, but she did not count to stand by and watch. She travelled the world in search of Osiris' corpse in order to pay him the tributes paid to the dead. With the help of her sister Nephthys, Isis went in search of her late husband. On her journey, she met children who told her that they had seen the tomb vault in which Osiris was lying. Following their recommendations, Isis followed the course of the river to at its mouth, listening only to its heart and guided by the wind that pushed the chest through the waters, Isis crossed the swamps of the delta reaching the sea. Along the coasts she was guided as if by her late husband to the city of Byblos, where the chest of Osiris had failed against a tree. In contact with the divine nature of the sarcophagus, this tamarisk grows to swallow it up completely. Discovering this immense marvel, the king of Byblos decided to cut the tree down to make it a pillar of his palace. Isis went to the king's palace to find her husband, posing as an ordinary human, Isis became a nanny to the queen's son and as if to her own son, she gave him her finger and the night burned everything that the child had that was deadly. But in front of this ritual, the queen interrupted the scene, it was then that the sovereign goddess regained her divine and dazzling appearance. She obtained by this occasion the chest in which she was able to found her husband, and left the king the column that became an object of veneration.
Isis in the form of a bird carrying an ankh (A Cross of Life), accompanied by his sister in the same form, opened Osiris' coffin. They both flapped their wings over the inanimate body of the greatest of the gods, to direct the air towards the Osiris' nostrils and bring him back to life. However, it was not enough. Isis whispered to her late husband these few words: "Osiris, see, your sister Isis came, your wife, opening herself to your love. Place it on your phallus so that whatever comes out of your descendants will be in it." Osiris was partially revived and she was then able to enjoy one last time her phallus and therefore her semen which could thus fertilize her.

Isis became pregnant but the world was shaking: this union was not natural and should not have taken place, which caused a disruption These reunions were so devastating that the sky was torn apart and lightning struck at night, the gods themselves became afraid of the situation. Isis was pregnant and Horus was to be born of a dead father and rule over the lands of Egypt. The birth of Horus had to remain secret to avoid Seth's retaliation, but one night he discovered Osiris' chest open and entered in a deep rage. He cut Osiris' body into 14 parts. The pieces of the corpse were scattered all over Egypt.

Isis' second quest and the first mummy

Isis, learning of her vile brother's forfeiture, set out on a new quest: to collect her husband's dismembered pieces. With each discovery, she built a temple in honour of Osiris. But in this quest she was not alone, Nephthys was she also present and it is even said that Horus, still a child, helped to recover his father's parts. Isis would have managed to recover all the parts of her late husband's body, except perhaps her manly limb that Seth would have given in fish feed. Anubis was responsible for the mummification and wrapped the pieces of Osiris in a papyrus envelope called "nebride". The god with the jackal's head carefully dried the corpse, softened it with ointments, wrapped it in linen swaddling clothes end. It was the first mummy. The moods were harvested in the Senu vase.

Isis said these words to her husband: "Osiris, live and spread life around you. And you, lymph of this luminous body, deaf, flow, fill the canals, make yourself a river, quench our thirst. ".Then we saw the moods coming out of his body that went to sow the fields, swell the waters of the Nile and make the cereals germinate. After reigning over the world of the living, Osiris left to reign over the world of the dead. When he got there, he received the scepter, flypaper and shepherd's crook from Thoth's hands. He then became the ruler of the world of the dead, the "land of silence".
Osiris' body was buried in a sacred place, forbidden to men and where only Isis went by boat. Offers of offerings were always made in honour and memory of the one who brought peace and prosperity to the two countries. The world on which reigned Osiris now was only rarely frequented by the gods, only Thoth and Anubis played the role of intermediaries. Despite his absence on earth, Osiris was present in the luminous form of the Orion Nebula.

Horus' hidden childhood

Isis, pregnant with Osiris' last breath, knew that Seth would never let the rightful heir to the throne live. She took refuge in the marshes of Chemmis, deep in the delta, where papyrus and reeds formed a natural barrier against her brother's watchful eye. It was there, hidden from the world, that she gave birth to Horus — not to be confused with his uncle Horus the Elder, one of Nut's five children. This new Horus grew up far from the other gods, nursed by his mother and protected day and night by her magic, while Seth ruled Egypt from the throne he had stolen from his brother.

Isis and the seven scorpions

The young god's childhood was not a peaceful one, however. One day, as Isis fled through the delta with her son, seven scorpions joined her retinue to protect her: Tefen, Befen, Mestet, Mestetef, Petet, Thetet, and Matet. As evening fell, they reached a village where a wealthy woman, frightened by this procession of scorpions, slammed her door in their faces. A poor peasant woman named Taha, however, offered Isis and the child shelter.
Enraged by the wealthy woman's hostile welcome, the seven scorpions pooled their venom into the stinger of one of their number, Tefen, who slipped into the house during the night and stung the woman's young son. The child was seized with convulsions, and no one came to his weeping mother's aid, so widely despised was she for her hard-heartedness. Moved nonetheless by her cries, Isis took pity on the innocent child: she laid her hands upon him and spoke the magic formula naming each of the seven scorpions in turn, commanding their venom to leave his body. The child was saved, and the wealthy woman, ashamed of her conduct, gave all her possessions to poor Taha, who alone had known how to welcome the goddess.
This episode endured in Egyptian memory as proof of Isis' healing power: the formula she spoke that day was copied for centuries onto magical stelae, such as the famous Metternich Stela, consulted by the ancient Egyptians to treat scorpion stings.

The trials of the divine tribunal

Once grown, Horus appeared before the assembled Ennead to claim his father's throne. Seth objected at once: only he, he argued, had the strength to protect Egypt and to fend off Apophis every night aboard Re's barque. The tribunal of the gods, presided over by Re himself, hesitated for eighty years without reaching a verdict, favouring first one, then the other.
The two rivals then clashed directly, through cunning as much as through strength. Turned into hippopotamuses, they plunged into the Nile for a duel that Isis, fearing for her son's life, interrupted with an ill-fated harpoon that wounded Horus before she drew it back out, ashamed. Seth, furious at her interference, tore out his nephew's eyes and buried them; Hathor found them and healed them with gazelle's milk, restoring Horus' sight and, with it, the Eye of Horus — a symbol of protection and wholeness still found on amulets and sarcophagi today.
One final trial was arranged: a race of stone boats. Horus tricked his rival by secretly swapping his own boat for one of wood coated in plaster, which floated, while Seth's, carved from the true stone of a mountain, sank at once.

The final judgement and Horus' coronation

Weary of these endless quarrels, the tribunal was still on the verge of granting the throne to Seth when Osiris, from the kingdom of the dead, sent the gods a threatening letter: if Horus, his rightful son, was not crowned, he would send his messengers — the baleful spirits of the underworld — to demand justice among the living. Faced with this threat, Re and the Ennead finally reached their verdict: Horus was proclaimed the rightful king of the Two Lands, heir to his father.
Seth, however, was not destroyed: he was taken aboard Re's solar barque, where his brute strength was now put to use fending off Apophis, the serpent of chaos, every night — finding his place at last in the order of the world he had once tried to break. Horus, now crowned, became the model for every pharaoh who would reign over Egypt: each living king would henceforth be his earthly incarnation, while Osiris, from the afterlife, watched over the dead.
