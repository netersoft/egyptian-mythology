The Sun God

A god whose form changes with the hours of the day and night, Re is above all indispensable to life on Earth, so much so that he was placed at the head of the Great Ennead of the nine principal gods. Re is associated with Amun, the sun god of the city of Thebes, and takes the name Amun-Re when he stands at the height of his glory in the sky. He is the source of the whole Egyptian pantheon, and the pharaohs claimed descent from him, expecting to be united with him at their death. When Re was born, another creature appeared as well: Apophis, the giant serpent. The two were enemies from the start, one standing for the Sun and light in its life-giving sense, the other for darkness. While the serpent was banished to the darkness, Re was given the place of Atum: king of gods and men. From then on his eye watches over every land in the world. He is most often shown as a falcon-headed man crowned with the solar disc, in which form he is called Re-Horakhty.

The solar barque on the river of the sky

By day, Re sails the river of the sky in his solar barque, changing as the sun changes. His course runs from the eastern horizon to the west, arching over the earth. The journey along the river of the sky lasts twelve hours, from dawn to dusk, and Re's transformations follow the cycle of life: first a child, then a grown man and finally an old man.

As the day ends, Re, accompanied by Heka (protective magic) and Sia (knowledge), and sometimes by other gods such as Hu (the creative word) or Wepwawet (the opener of the ways), leaves the day behind and crosses the Duat (the afterlife) in the solar barque to reach the realm of night. During this crossing, Thoth takes over the governance of the sky.

During the first and second hours, the water is plentiful and the barque makes good progress. Baboons give a warm welcome to the gods, who are guarded by armed spirits.

During the third hour, the serpent Apophis appears and blocks Re's path. Weakened though he is at this hour of the night, the sun god manages to overcome him. Sometimes, however, he fails: the sun is then trapped in darkness (this may be the Egyptian explanation for solar eclipses). In this struggle Re can receive help, and he sometimes takes on the form of Atum to increase his strength.

At the fourth hour, the darkness is deeper than ever and the water has almost run out, so the barque has to be hauled across the sand to move forward. Once again, the serpent Apophis is to blame: he has swallowed all the water of the river. Fortunately, Re is not alone. This time it is Seth, usually known for his malice, who comes to his ancestor's aid. He pierces the serpent's flank with his spear and releases all the river's water so that the barque can continue on its way.

During the fifth hour, the barque passes close to the body of Osiris, and hope is at last reborn. Re then joins with his former body, the scarab Khepri, and so sets the cycle going again.

The sixth hour marks the return of Re's vital energy. To recover it, he must regenerate in a place safe from danger, and he is guarded by the benevolent serpent Mehen, who shelters him within his coils.

During the seventh and eighth hours, humans can at last catch sight of their god. They bring gifts to the riverbanks (weapons, fabrics, royal regalia). But others think differently and try to attack the sun god. They are immediately captured and punished: tied to torture posts and burned by fire-spitting cobras. Some are beheaded and boiled in great cauldrons, and their blood feeds the surrounding lakes of fire. Meanwhile, Apophis is not done with Re and tries to strike again, but he is stopped at once by the goddess Serket (or Serqet), who strikes him down in fury.

From the ninth hour to the eleventh, the sun begins to climb. Apophis makes his great return for a final battle between light and darkness. He tries once more to stop time, but fails, because Re has almost regained his full power.

At the twelfth hour, the barque is hauled up towards the light. Passing through the serpent of time, those aboard turn from old men back into children, and all the world's inhabitants receive the light: they are called the "flocks of Re".

The destruction of mankind

As time went on, Re grew old, and mankind, forgetting the respect they owed him, began to plot against his authority. Enraged by this betrayal, the sun god gathered the council of the gods and decided to send his Eye down to earth, in the form of the goddess Hathor, to punish the rebels. She then transformed into the bloodthirsty lioness Sekhmet and began slaughtering humanity with such violence that the fields ran red with blood. Overcome with remorse at the scale of the carnage, Re wanted to stop her, but Sekhmet, caught up in a murderous frenzy, refused to obey.
The gods had to resort to a trick: they had an immense quantity of beer dyed red and poured it over the flooded fields before the goddess arrived. Mistaking it for blood, Sekhmet drank it greedily until she grew drunk and fell asleep, forgetting her rage and thereby saving the rest of humanity. Upon waking, at peace once more, she resumed Hathor's benevolent form. Exhausted by this episode, Re decided to leave the earth for good: he climbed onto the back of the celestial cow Nut and rose into the sky, leaving the other gods, and later the pharaohs, to govern humanity directly from then on.

Re's secret name

As he aged, Re would sometimes let a little saliva escape at the corner of his mouth. Isis, a cunning goddess determined to uncover the secrets of divine power, gathered this saliva mixed with dust and shaped it into a venomous serpent, which she placed in the god's path. Bitten, Re was seized with pain so intense that none of the gods, for all their knowledge, could relieve it.
Isis then came forward, offering to heal him on condition that he reveal his true secret name to her — the source of all his power, unknown even to the other gods. Re first tried to deceive her by listing his many titles and epithets, but the poison kept consuming him. Finally, at the end of his strength, he agreed to entrust his name to Isis, on the sole condition that she pass it on only to her son Horus. Isis then healed the god, but she had gained immense power of her own, now hers to pass down through her line.
