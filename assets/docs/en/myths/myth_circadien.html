The Sun God

A polymorphic God according to the time of day and night, Re is indispensable to life on Earth, so that it was placed at the top of the Great Ennead of the nine principal gods. Re is associated with Amun, the sun god of the city of Thebes, who calls himself Amun-Re when he is at the height of his glory in heaven. He is the originator of the entire Egyptian pantheon and is the one to whom the Pharaohs are attached, who upon their death When Re was born, another creature also appeared: Apophis, the giant snake. They automatically become enemies, one representing the Sun and light in its beneficial sense, the other is darkness. While the snake is relegated to darkness, Re is offered the place of Tem: the one king of gods and men. His eye now watches over all the regions of the world. He is represented by mostly in the form of a falcon-headed man, surmounted by the solar disk called Horakhty.

The journey of the solar boat on the celestial river

Re during the day travels the river of heaven in the solar boat and transforms itself, in the image of the sun. This path runs from the eastern horizon to the west, passing over the earth in a long curve. The trip on the river of heaven lasts 12 hours from night to afternoon and Re's transformations follow the cycle of life: first as a child, then as a perfectly adult and finally as an old man.

During the last hours of the day, Re accompanied by Heqa (the protective magic) and Sia (knowledge) and sometimes other gods like Hou (the creative verb) or Upuaut. (the god of paths) crosses the Douat (The Hereafter) in the solar boat to reach the kingdom of the night by leaving the day. During the crossing it is Thoth who takes over for the governance of the heaven.

During the first and second hour, the water is abundant and the boat progresses well. Baboons welcome warmly to the gods who are protected by armed geniuses.

During the third hour, the snake Apophis appears and gets in the way of the road of Re. Even weakens at this time of night, the sun god succeeds in overcoming him. Only sometimes, this is not the case: the sun is then trapped in darkness (this may be the Egyptian explanation of the phenomenon of solar eclipse). In his struggle, Re can be helped and can sometimes take the form of Tem to increase his power.

At the fourth hour, the darkness is deeper than ever and the water is no longer really abundant, so you have to pull the boat to get her to move forward on the sand. It is again the fault of the snake Apophis who swallowed all the water from the river. Fortunately, Re is not alone. This time, it is the god Seth, usually known for his malice, who comes to help his ancestor. He pierces the flank of the snake with his spear and releases all the water from the river so that the boat can resume its course.

During the fifth hour, the boat passes by Osiris' corpse and hope is finally reborn. Re then joins forces with his former corpse, the beetle Khepri and thus restarts the cycle.

The sixth hour marks the return of Re's vital energy. To do this, it must regenerate itself in a safe environment. It is then protected by the beneficial snake Mehen who protects it by wrapping it in its rings.

During the seventh and eighth hour, Men can finally see their god. They bring gifts on the banks of the river (weapons, fabrics, royal attributes). But others disagree and try to attack the sun god. They are automatically captured and castelled, tied to torture poles and burned by cobras flame-spitting devices. Some of them are decapitated and put to cook in large pots. Their blood comes to feed the surrounding lakes of flames. Meanwhile, Apophis has not finished with Re and tries to come back to the charge, but he is directly arrested by the goddess Serket (or Serqet) who takes him down with fury.

From the ninth hour until the eleventh, the sun begins to be high in the sky. Apophis makes his big comeback for a final confrontation between light and darkness. Apophis is still trying to stop time, but is unable to do so because Re is almost at the height of his power.

At the twelfth hour, the boat is raised towards the light. As they cross the snake of time, they move from old people to children. Thus all the inhabitants receive the light: they are called the "herds of Re".

The destruction of mankind

As time went on, Re grew old, and mankind, forgetting the respect they owed him, began to plot against his authority. Enraged by this betrayal, the sun god gathered the council of the gods and decided to send his Eye down to earth, in the form of the goddess Hathor, to punish the rebels. She then transformed into the bloodthirsty lioness Sekhmet and began slaughtering humanity with such violence that the fields ran red with blood. Overcome with remorse at the scale of the carnage, Re wanted to stop her, but Sekhmet, caught up in a murderous frenzy, refused to obey.
The gods had to resort to a trick: they had an immense quantity of beer dyed red and poured it over the flooded fields before the goddess arrived. Mistaking it for blood, Sekhmet drank it greedily until she grew drunk and fell asleep, forgetting her rage and thereby saving the rest of humanity. Upon waking, at peace once more, she resumed Hathor's benevolent form. Exhausted by this episode, Re decided to leave the earth for good: he climbed onto the back of the celestial cow Nut and rose into the sky, leaving the other gods, and later the pharaohs, to govern humanity directly from then on.

Re's secret name

As he aged, Re would sometimes let a little saliva escape at the corner of his mouth. Isis, a cunning goddess determined to uncover the secrets of divine power, gathered this saliva mixed with dust and shaped it into a venomous serpent, which she placed in the god's path. Bitten, Re was seized with pain so intense that none of the gods, for all their knowledge, could relieve it.
Isis then came forward, offering to heal him on condition that he reveal his true secret name to her — the source of all his power, unknown even to the other gods. Re first tried to deceive her by listing his many titles and epithets, but the poison kept consuming him. Finally, at the end of his strength, he agreed to entrust his name to Isis, on the sole condition that she pass it on only to her son Horus. Isis then healed the god, but she had gained immense power of her own, now hers to pass down through her line.
